## V6.1 — Skills: instructions Claude gives itself (5 min)

🟦 GAMMA OUTLINE
```
Title: From prompts to Skills
Slide: Project = WHO you are. Skill = HOW to do a specific job.
Slide: Skills auto-trigger when your request matches — global, across Chat & Cowork
Slide: A skill = a folder with a SKILL.md
Slide: Prompt = you remember/find/paste. Skill = it just activates.
```

NARRATION
> Over five weeks you've built the pieces. This week we turn them into a system that keeps getting more valuable — and it starts with Skills.
>
> A skill is a set of instructions Claude reads automatically when your request matches what the skill does. If a Project tells Claude *who you are*, a skill tells Claude *how to do a specific job*. Skills are global — they work across every project, in Chat and Cowork — and they trigger themselves; you don't have to activate them.
>
> Here's why a skill beats a saved prompt: a prompt is something you have to remember exists, find, paste, and adapt every time. A skill just activates the moment the situation matches. If you keep explaining the same thing to Claude, or running the same process over and over, that's a skill waiting to be built.

## V6.2 — The 3 ways to build a skill (4 min)

🟦 GAMMA OUTLINE
```
Title: Three ways to build
Slide: Method 1 — build upfront from a good prompt/SOP
Slide: Method 2 — do the work first, then convert (BEST — captures what you actually did)
Slide: Method 3 — off-the-shelf (variable quality, not customised)
```

NARRATION
> There are three ways to build a skill. Method one: build it upfront, if you already have a reliable prompt or a clear process. Method two — the best one — do the workflow first with Claude, all the way to an output you're happy with, then ask Claude to extract the principles and turn it into a skill. Method two wins because the skill captures what you *actually* did, not what you thought you'd do. Method three is off-the-shelf — install someone else's skill; great as a starting point, but quality varies and it's not customised to you.

## V6.5 — Efficiency vs. Innovation AI: your next builds (4 min)

🟦 GAMMA OUTLINE
```
Title: Where to point your AI next
Slide: Efficiency AI — speed up what you already do (low-hanging fruit)
Slide: Innovation AI — things that weren't practical before (bigger wins)
Slide: Opportunity table — time saved · what's needed · setup time → pick TOP 3
```

NARRATION
> Your audit surfaced gaps — now decide which to close first. There are two kinds of AI wins. Efficiency AI speeds up tasks you already do — easy to see, real but incremental. Innovation AI does things that weren't practical before — a daily news briefing, a personalised proposal for every lead, a strategy doc that updates itself. Most people default to efficiency because it's obvious, but the biggest gains usually come from innovation — the things you weren't doing at all. Fill in the opportunity table, weigh time saved against setup effort, and pick your top three. That's your roadmap for the next month.
